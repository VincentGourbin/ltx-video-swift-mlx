## What DFR actually needed

Auditing DFR against this port before writing anything: its "spatial detailing
LoRA" for LTX-2.5 **is** `LTX-2.5-22b-IC-LoRA-Pixel-Spatial-Upscaler`, the
adapter the `upscale` command already fuses, down to the
`reference_downscale_factor: 2` this port already reads from its metadata.
Upstream's stage 2 (`stage.with_loras(... detailing_lora)`, full-resolution
re-denoise conditioned on the half-resolution reference) is the same operation
as our two-stage chain. Nothing to port.

The genuinely missing primitive was `VideoGeneratedKeyframeSlots`.

## What a slot is

An anchor the model **invents**, at a pixel frame we choose, generated by the
same pass that generates the video. Stage 2 refines it at full resolution;
temporal tiles then condition on it. That is what holds one identity across a
seam, where anchoring on a decoded neighbour frame drifts — the failure already
recorded in [[smoothness-metrics-miss-identity-drift]].

## Why not reuse the appended guide tokens as they were

This package already appends keyframe tokens ([[keyframes-append-not-inject]]).
A slot is that mechanism with three properties changed, and all three have to
hold together:

1. **Denoised, not frozen.** A guide carries timestep 0 and its predicted
   velocity is discarded. A slot carries the schedule's current sigma and its
   velocity is stepped exactly like the video latent's. Upstream expresses this
   as `denoise_mask = 1`, which also means any content handed in is lerped
   toward noise rather than kept clean.
2. **Marked.** The checkpoint's learned `keyframes_abs_pos_embedding` is added,
   right after `patchify_proj`, to the slot's tokens. It is what distinguishes a
   token representing *one pixel frame* from one representing an eight-frame
   span. A slot denoised without it is off-distribution, not merely unmarked.
3. **One pixel frame of RoPE span**, `[t, t+1)`, against a latent frame's eight.

Slots are appended after any frozen guides, so the sequence reads
`[video | guides | slots]` and the marked, denoised span is always trailing and
contiguous — which is what lets one range serve both the marker and the
per-token timestep.

## Compatibility

The marker is declared on both transformers and initialised to zeros, so a
checkpoint that never shipped one adds nothing to any token: pre-2.5
generations are bit-identical. Requesting slots on such a checkpoint is refused
from the config alone, before any model is loaded.

## Verification

Slot positions are pinned against Lightricks' own `_slot_positions` via
`scripts/keyframe_slot_reference.py`: temporal coordinates and the full spatial
grid match to 1e-5. The port's own round trip (build → tokens → unpack) is exact.

End to end, a real run wrote `[1, 128, 2, 16, 24]` for two slots at 768x512 —
the shape the layout predicts — and each slot decodes to the scene at its own
frame. The number that makes that readable is the control: a *real* frame of the
same clip, put through the same one-latent-frame VAE round trip, comes back at
16.01 dB against itself, and the slot scores 16.18 dB against that frame. The
slot is as close to the frame as the VAE's own round trip is; the softness both
share is the decoder's behaviour on a latent spanning one pixel frame instead of
eight. Artefacts: `docs/examples/ltx-2.5/keyframe-slots/`.

Cost: 242 s with two slots against baselines of 187.6 s and 229.0 s at the same
seed and geometry. Two slots add 12.5% tokens, and the spread between the two
identical baselines is as large as the difference — small, and not separable
from run-to-run variance at this sample size.
